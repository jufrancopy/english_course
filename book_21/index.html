Las 5 Reglas de Oro para Aprobar el Book 21

1. Subjuntivo Mandatorio (L4): Tras suggest / recommend / urge that... el verbo SIEMPRE va en forma base: "I suggest that he follow" (NUNCA follows, followed o will follow).
2. Such vs So (L2): Usa such cuando hay sustantivo: such a mess, such slow trains. Usa so cuando solo hay adjetivo/adverbio: so difficult, so fast.
3. Prohibición con Gerundio (L3): La estructura de prohibición oficial de este libro es There will be no + -ing: "There will be no arguing in the class".
4. Too + Adjetivo + To-Infinitive (L1): Expresa exceso con efecto negativo: "He drove too fast to avoid the accident" o "Too softly for us to hear".
5. Sinónimos Clave de Examen: Recruit = join/enlist | Discharge = leave the military | Against the law = illegal | Conclude = finish | Drain = empty water.
6. Cláusulas Reducidas a Infinitivo (L4): "Letters that I should mail" se convierte en "letters to mail". "Papers that Mr. Smith must sign" -> "papers for him to sign".

Lección 1 (Book 21 Página 1)

The Most Beautiful Place: Comparativos, Superlativos y 'Too...To'

Comparativos y Superlativos de Adjetivos y Adverbios

En Book 21 se revisan las formas regulares e irregulares de adjetivos y adverbios, prestando especial atención a far → farther → the farthest y adjetivos de más de dos sílabas.

Tipo / Palabra Positivo Comparativo (+ than) Superlativo (the +)
Distancia (Irregular) far farther than the farthest
Tiempo (Adverbio) early earlier than the earliest
Una sílaba high higher than the highest
Dos o más sílabas careful / beautiful more careful than the most careful

Estructura: Too + Adverbio/Adjetivo + To-Infinitive

Expresa que una cantidad o grado es excesivo para que una acción pueda ocurrir (consecuencia negativa implícita).

FÓRMULA: Sujeto + Verbo + too + Adjetivo/Adverbio + to-Infinitive (+ complemento)
Ejemplo 1 (Adjetivo): "We arrived too late to see the beginning of the movie."
(Llegamos demasiado tarde como para ver el inicio).
Ejemplo 2 (Adverbio con sujeto for): "Mike spoke too softly for us to hear him."
(Mike habló demasiado bajo como para que lo oyéramos).

Indirect Speech: Reporte de Pasado Progresivo

Cuando alguien dice una oración en pasado progresivo (was/were + -ing), al reportarla cambia a pasado perfecto progresivo (had been + -ing).

Directo: "I was visiting France when I ran into friends."
Reportado: He said that he HAD BEEN VISITING France...
Lección 2 (Book 21 Página 45)

He's Tired As A Result Of...: Causa, Efecto y Pasiva

Cláusulas de Resultado: So...That vs Such...That

Uso de SO

Se usa antes de adjetivos o adverbios solos (o con many/much/few/little).

so + ADJ/ADV + that
so much / so many + NOUN + that

"The desk is so messy that he can't work."
"It rained so much that we needed boats."

Uso de SUCH

Se usa cuando acompaña a un sustantivo (con o sin adjetivo delante).

such + a/an + ADJ + SINGULAR NOUN + that
such + (ADJ) + PLURAL/NONCOUNT + that

"Jack's desk is such a mess that he can't work."
"It was such an interesting city that I'll return."

Conectores de Causa y Efecto

Conector Significado Ejemplo Oficial ALC
As a result Como resultado "The street was flooded. As a result, it was barricaded."
Therefore Por lo tanto "Mary has a fever. Therefore, she won't be able to sing."
Consequently Por consiguiente "Bruce didn't study; consequently, he failed the exam."
Thus Así / Por ende "The crowd began to yell; thus it was impossible to hear."

Sufijos de Sustantivo (-y, -ty, -ity, -ility)

Al añadir estos sufijos a adjetivos, se crean los sustantivos abstractos de propiedad o estado:

flexible → flexibility
elastic → elasticity
secure → security
equal → equality
Lección 3 (Book 21 Página 80)

It's A Quiet Place Where I Can Study: Relativos y Gerundios

Pronombres Relativos (Relative Pronouns)

Pronombre Uso para... Ejemplo de Examen
who Personas (sujeto) "The lady in the red dress who is talking to Mr. Smith."
whose Posesión (de quién es) "We met a woman whose husband is a general."
where Lugares físicos "Do you remember the shop where you bought that jacket?"
when Tiempo / momento "Do you know the time when the movie begins?"
which / that Cosas o animales "The book that Bill was telling us about."

Pasado Perfecto en Voz Pasiva

Acción pasiva completada antes de un momento específico o antes de otra acción en pasado:

FÓRMULA: had been + past participle (participio pasado)

"The decision had been made before he left the office."
"The barracks had been painted before the recruits moved in."

Prohibición con Gerundio: 'There will be no + -ing'

Estructura militar formal para prohibir una conducta terminantemente:

There will be no + VERB-ING

"There will be no arguing in the class."
"There will be no smoking in the dormitory."

Lección 4 (Book 21 Página 131)

I Suggest You Re-Enlist: El Subjuntivo y Cláusulas Reducidas

Subjuntivo con Verbos de Recomendación y Sugerencia

Tras verbos como suggest, recommend, urge, insist, require seguidos de la cláusula that, el verbo subordinado debe ir en forma base (infinitivo sin to), sin importar el sujeto (incluso con he/she/it no lleva -s).

Sujeto + suggest/recommend/urge + that + Sujeto 2 + VERBO BASE
✅ "I suggest that he follow the chain of command." (NUNCA follows ni will follow)
✅ "Her mother recommended that she find a different dentist." (NUNCA finds ni found)
✅ "The doctor urged that she get a second opinion." (NUNCA gets)

Cláusulas Relativas Reducidas a To-Infinitive

Una cláusula con relativo + modal se reduce a un infinitivo de propósito:

Forma Completa:

"Here are some letters that I should mail."

Forma Reducida:

"Here are some letters to mail."

Con Sujeto (for + pronombre):

"Here are papers that Mr. Smith must sign."

Forma Reducida:

"Here are papers for him to sign."

Infinitivo como Complemento de Sujeto (Duty, Goal, Advice)

Cuando el sujeto es un deber, meta o consejo, el predicado nominal usa to + verbo:

• "The airman's duty is to follow orders."
• "Kevin's goal is to get a promotion."
• "The boss's advice was to look for a new job."
Lección 5 / Apéndice (Book 21 Página 81)

Troublesome English: Sustantivos Contables vs No Contables

Muchas palabras en inglés cambian de significado según se usen como Non-count (concepto o sustancia general) o como Count (unidad particular o evento individual):

Palabra Uso No Contable (General) Uso Contable (Unidad Específica)
activity There was much activity going on. They planned many summer activities.
light Plants need more light to grow. Do you have a light / turn on the lights.
agreement Agreement was prevalent among us. We signed an official lease agreement.
time I wish I had more time to read. We went there three times.
